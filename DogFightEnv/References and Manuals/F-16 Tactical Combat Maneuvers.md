The operational F-16 Fighting Falcon executes maneuvers categorized into **Tactical Combat Maneuvers (Basic Fighter Maneuvers / BFM)** used in aerial warfare and **Aerobatic Demonstration Maneuvers** performed by teams like the USAF Viper Demo Team and Thunderbirds.

### **Tactical Combat Maneuvers (BFM)**

* **High-G Break Turn:** An instantaneous or sustained turn pulling up to 9.0 Gs to turn tightly inside an enemy's flight path or defeat an incoming missile lock.  
* **High Yo-Yo:** A climb into the vertical plane during an offensive chase to bleed off excess speed, preventing an overshoot while keeping tactical positioning above the enemy.  
* **Low Yo-Yo:** A shallow dive toward the inside of a turn to temporarily exchange altitude for airspeed, enabling the F-16 to close distance on a maneuvering bandit.  
* **Immelmann Turn:** A vertical half-loop upward ending with a half-roll at the crest, trading speed for altitude while reversing flight direction by 180°.  
* **Split-S:** A half-roll inverted followed by a pulling dive out the bottom, trading altitude for rapid acceleration to disengage or change heading.  
* **Barrel Roll Attack:** A helical maneuver combining a roll and a loop to displace the F-16 sideways off a defender's flight line without surrendering airspeed.  
* **Flat & Rolling Scissors:** A series of alternating cross-turns or rolling passes where two opposing fighters attempt to force each other to fly out in front (overshoot).  
* **Defensive Spiral:** A steep, tight diving spiral near the maximum elevator limit designed to drain an attacking jet's energy or break a gun track.

### **Aerobatic & Demonstration Maneuvers**

* **Unrestricted Vertical Climb:** Pitching straight up into a 90° pure vertical vector immediately after takeoff, utilizing the F-16's greater than 1:1 thrust-to-weight ratio to climb thousands of feet per minute.  
* **Maximum Alpha (High AoA) Pass:** Slow flight at approximately 100–120 knots with the nose pitched up at the computer-enforced 25° Angle of Attack limit.  
* **Knife-Edge Pass:** Level flight banked sideways at 90°, using continuous rudder yaw authority to counteract gravity and maintain altitude.  
* **Point Hesitation Rolls (4-Point & 8-Point):** Axial rolls paused abruptly every 90° or 45°, highlighting the fly-by-wire system's crisp roll rate (up to 300° per second).  
* **Falcon Turn (Minimum Radius Turn):** A continuous, 360-degree hard turn at maximum G-load to showcase the jet's absolute tightest turn circle.  
* **Tactical Overhead Pitch / Break to Land:** A high-speed approach over the airfield where the pilot rolls hard past 60° bank to shed speed rapidly before landing.

## II. Offensive Gun Employment Maneuvers

### High-G Break Turn

Tactical Purpose: Force the defender to fly out in front (overshoot) so you can roll in behind for a tracking shot.

In a gun fight, the break turn is used offensively when you are in the defender's control zone but need to tighten your turn radius to prevent an overshoot. By pulling 9G at corner velocity, you reduce your turn radius to \~2,000 ft and increase turn rate to \~20°/sec. This keeps your nose inside the defender's flight path while bleeding just enough energy to stay behind them. If you overshoot, you become the defender.

*"The turn rate of the F-16 is around 20 degrees per second at 15,000 feet MSL...about 10 seconds"* for a 180° turn.

### High Yo-Yo

Tactical Purpose: Prevent overshoot while maintaining the "high perch" for a gun solution.

In a gun fight, the worst outcome is flying past the defender (a flight path overshoot). The high yo-yo converts horizontal closure into vertical displacement. You climb above the defender's plane of motion, reducing horizontal velocity while keeping your lift vector aligned for a subsequent diving gun run. This is critical because guns require you to be behind the target; an overshoot puts you in front, where you are vulnerable.

*"The high yo-yo is also useful for preventing overshoots and reducing AOT...uses three-dimensional maneuvering rather than increased load factor to reduce horizontal turn radius."*

### Low Yo-Yo

Tactical Purpose: Close the distance and gain angular advantage when you have an energy surplus but poor nose position.

If you are outside the defender's turn circle (too far for a gun solution), the low yo-yo trades altitude for airspeed and uses gravity to accelerate into the inside of the defender's turn. This reduces range and angle-off-tail (AOT) rapidly. In gun combat, being inside the bandit's turn circle is essential—you cannot shoot what you cannot point your nose at.

### Barrel Roll Attack

Tactical Purpose: Execute a lag displacement roll to move from a lag pursuit position to a lead pursuit position without overshooting.

This is one of the most important gun-fighting maneuvers. If you are in pure pursuit (nose pointed at the bandit) but closing too fast, a barrel roll moves your aircraft laterally off the defender's flight line while maintaining energy. The result is a virtual 90° course change using all three dimensions, placing you in lead pursuit with the pipper on target.

*"The barrel roll attack uses a much tighter loop than the roll, completing a full loop while only executing 3/4 of a roll. The result is a virtual 90 degree turn, using all three dimensions."*

---

## III. Defensive Gun Evasion Maneuvers

### High-G Break Turn (Defensive)

Tactical Purpose: Defeat a gun track by maximizing turn rate and forcing the attacker to overshoot.

When a bandit is tracking you with guns, the defensive break is an aggressive, maximum onset-rate pull into the attack. At 9G and \~20°/sec turn rate, you rotate your aircraft's vulnerable axis away from the gun line faster than the attacker can correct. If timed correctly, the attacker will either:

* Lose the pipper and have to re-acquire (buying you 2–3 seconds)  
* Overshoot and become the defender  
* Bleed so much energy in the attempt that you gain a sustained turn advantage

*"An aggressive symmetric, maximum onset rate, two-hand, stick in the lap pull and hold until the aircraft aerodynamically stalls."*

### Defensive Spiral

Tactical Purpose: Break a sustained gun track by exploiting the vertical plane and energy differential.

If the attacker is close behind and has a stable gun solution, a level turn may not be enough. The defensive spiral is a steep, tight diving spiral that forces the attacker to either:

* Follow you into the dive and bleed energy trying to maintain G  
* Overshoot above you due to closure rate  
* Lose sight in the high-G, rapidly changing geometry

*"The defensive spiral is a maneuver you'll use if you're at low airspeed and the bandits in close behind you and you have a lot of altitude available."*

### Flat Scissors

Tactical Purpose: Force an overshoot in a slow-speed, co-planar engagement.

When two fighters merge at similar speeds and enter a nose-to-nose contest, the flat scissors is a turn-radius fight. Each pilot reverses turn direction, trying to get behind the other. In a gun fight, the scissors is dangerous because it keeps both aircraft in the same plane of motion—perfect for a snapshot. The key is to deny the opponent a stable track by reversing across their nose before they can fire.

*"The flat scissors is the simpler of the two to explain...results when two fighters of similar capability encounter each other at similar speeds and in the same plane of motion."*

### Rolling Scissors

Tactical Purpose: Escape a gun solution by introducing three-dimensional geometry.

If you are in a flat scissors and the opponent is gaining a tracking advantage, converting to a rolling scissors introduces vertical displacement. The corkscrew geometry makes it nearly impossible for the attacker to maintain a steady gun line because the target is constantly moving in three axes. However, this is a desperation maneuver—it bleeds energy from both aircraft and often ends in a stalemate or a low-altitude recovery.

---

## IV. Transitional / Disengagement Maneuvers

### Immelmann Turn

Tactical Purpose: Reverse direction 180° while trading speed for altitude, useful after a high-speed gun pass.

If you make a high-speed gun pass and miss, you are now in front of the defender. The Immelmann converts your excess airspeed into altitude and reverses your heading, ideally placing you back in a perch position above the defender. In gun combat, this is risky because the time spent in the vertical is time the defender can use to reverse and shoot at your belly.

### Split-S

Tactical Purpose: Disengage or reverse heading while accelerating, useful when you have an altitude advantage.

The Split-S is a safer disengagement than the Immelmann in a gun fight because it keeps you moving toward the ground (and away from the defender's nose) while accelerating. If you are above the bandit and need to exit the fight or reposition, the Split-S uses gravity to rebuild energy while reversing course.

## VI. The Gun Fight Decision Matrix

Table

| Situation | Recommended Maneuver | Why |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| You are behind, closing too fast | High Yo-Yo or Barrel Roll Attack | Prevent overshoot; maintain perch |
| You are behind, outside turn circle | Low Yo-Yo | Close distance; gain angles |
| Bandit has gun track on you | Defensive Break Turn or Spiral | Max turn rate; break track |
| Nose-to-nose after merge | Flat Scissors (if slow) or Rolling Scissors (if fast) | Force overshoot; deny tracking |
| You overshot, bandit reversing | Immelmann or Split-S | Reorient; regain energy |
| Slow, bandit in control zone | Defensive Spiral | Vertical escape; force overshoot |

## VII. Key Tactical Principle: The "Tracking Shot" vs. "Snapshot"

In guns-only combat, there are two kill opportunities:

1. Tracking Shot: You are in a stable lead pursuit behind the defender. You pull the trigger and walk rounds into the target. This requires nose position and low closure rate—achieved via yo-yos and barrel roll attacks.  
2. Snapshot: A high-deflection pass where you fire at a crossing target and let the bullets intercept. This requires precise timing and is often the result of a scissors where both aircraft cross noses.

The maneuvers listed above are all designed to either:

* Create a tracking shot opportunity (offensive BFM)  
* Deny the opponent a tracking shot opportunity (defensive BFM)

*"The high yo-yo is also useful for preventing overshoots and reducing AOT...uses three-dimensional maneuvering rather than increased load factor to reduce horizontal turn radius."*

## Summary

In a close-range, guns-only dogfight, the F-16's maneuver set serves a single purpose: control the geometry so you can point your nose at the enemy before they can point theirs at you.

* Offensively, the High Yo-Yo, Barrel Roll Attack, and Low Yo-Yo prevent overshoots and create stable gun-tracking opportunities.  
* Defensively, the Break Turn, Defensive Spiral, and Scissors break the opponent's gun track and force them to fly out in front.  
* The Falcon Turn (minimum radius turn) is the foundation—if you can out-turn the bandit at 9G, you will eventually drive your nose to their flight path.

The aerobatic maneuvers (knife-edge, hesitation rolls, pure vertical climb) are demonstrations of control authority but offer no tactical advantage in a gun fight. The exception is high-alpha flight, which allows the F-16 to point its nose at extreme angles for a snapshot even when energy-depleted.
